## 1. $200k–$400k households: spend mix beats income

Two households both earn $300,000. One should pay $325 a year in card fees. The other can justify $1,220.

We modeled six wallets for three households and looked only at Year 2: no welcome bonus, every fee paid, credits counted only when they get used.

- Home-centered (groceries first, two trips a year): Amex Gold + a 2% card netted $2,634 a year. A Sapphire Reserve setup netted $1,751.
- Travel-heavy (monthly flights, prepaid luxury hotels): Amex Platinum + Gold + a 2% card netted $6,094.

Income tells you what you can get approved for. Spend mix tells you what pays.

Full math: https://bestcardsforme.com/articles/credit-card-strategy-200k-400k-household-income-2026

## 2. The best second card is rarely another premium card

If you already pay for a Platinum or Sapphire Reserve, the question isn't "what's the best card?" It's "what does a second card add to the wallet I already have?"

For a balanced household that transfers points:
- Reserve + Amex Gold: +$969 a year after Gold's fee
- Platinum + Amex Gold: +$1,375 a year

But if you cash points out at about 1¢, the winner flips to a plain $0 2% card, with Venture X close behind.

Your redemption habits pick the second card.

## 3. Annual fees stack. Benefits don't.

Add an Amex Platinum to a Sapphire Reserve wallet and fees go from $795 to $1,690.

What the second premium card is worth in our Year-2 model:
- Travel-heavy household: +$1,203 a year
- Balanced household: +$508
- Home-centered household: −$307

The reason is overlap. You can sit in only one lounge at a time, so we count lounge access once. A household that rarely flies would need about $832 of Platinum credits it actually uses just to break even.

https://bestcardsforme.com/articles/multiple-premium-credit-cards-vs-one-2026

## 4. Platinum vs Reserve: when holding both makes sense

The usual advice is "pick one." Our updated comparison puts numbers on the exception: holding both pays for households that fly often and book prepaid luxury hotels most years, because the Platinum's 5x on flights and $600 hotel credit land on spending that already happens.

Also updated: the Saks credit ended June 30, 2026, CLEAR+ is now up to $219, and the Reserve's direct-booking rate is 4x. Every card term is re-checked against issuer pages automatically.

## 5. High-spend LLCs: two business cards beat one

At $400,000 a year of business spend, with $150,000 in Ink Business Preferred's 3x categories, routing each dollar to the better card beats either card alone:
- Ink Preferred for travel, shipping, internet/phone and ads (3x up to $150,000)
- Spark Cash Plus for everything else (2%, $150 fee refunded at $150,000 of spend)

Modeled net: $12,105 a year on $95 of fees. The catch: if you cash out points at 1¢, Ink's 1x on everything else is worth less than 2%, so use Ink only for its bonus categories.

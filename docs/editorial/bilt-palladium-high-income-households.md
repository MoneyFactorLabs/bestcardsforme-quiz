# Is Bilt Palladium worth it for high-income households?

**Editorial draft · Terms checked November 19, 2026**

The $495 Bilt Palladium can be worth it if your eligible purchases earn meaningfully more than your existing cards or you would naturally use enough of its credits and lounge benefits. A six-figure income tells you little about either. Start with the card you would actually replace on each purchase and treat housing points as a separate decision.

## Compare Palladium with Obsidian

Palladium earns 2x on eligible everyday purchases. Obsidian earns 3x in your selected dining or grocery category, 2x on eligible travel, and 1x elsewhere. For grocery, the 3x rate is limited to the first $25,000 in eligible annual grocery spend. Both can use the same housing-only tiers. On the same eligible housing payment and qualifying card spend, the housing points cancel out in a comparison between the tiers.

At an illustrative 1¢ per Bilt Point, Palladium earns one extra cent per dollar of ordinary spend over Obsidian and gives up one cent per dollar in Obsidian’s eligible 3x category. Eligible travel at 2x cancels out. The annual fee difference is $400. Before credits, that means ordinary spend needs to exceed the eligible 3x spend by $40,000 to justify Palladium. The examples below assume spend in the 3x category stays within any applicable cap.

## Worked examples for three households

| Illustrative income | Eligible annual spend on the card | Extra purchase rewards from Palladium | Net vs. Obsidian after $400 fee difference, before benefits |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| $200,000 | $12,000 dining, $24,000 other | $120 | −$280 |
| $300,000 | $10,000 dining, $40,000 other | $300 | −$100 |
| $500,000 | $12,000 dining, $72,000 other | $600 | +$200 |

Income is context only. The $300,000 household needs $100 of additional usable value over Obsidian to break even. If it already has Priority Pass and expects little incremental value from Bilt Cash, Obsidian may be the better fit. The $500,000 household may prefer Palladium over Obsidian, yet a simple 2% card would match Palladium’s 2x on the same eligible everyday spend at a 1¢ valuation. The housing program and benefits then need to justify the fee against that baseline.

## Put a household value on the credits

Before the refresh, Palladium offers up to $400 in Bilt Travel hotel credits across two periods and $200 in annual Bilt Cash. Obsidian offers up to $100 in Bilt Travel hotel credits across two periods. After the refresh, Palladium gets $600 of annual Bilt Cash and Obsidian gets $100. Eligible Palladium hotel redemptions of up to $200 twice per year are uses of the $600 balance. Assign value only to a purchase you would have made anyway, net of any price premium or restriction.

The refreshed terms apply to new accounts from January 1, 2027. Earlier accounts generally switch at their first anniversary in 2027 unless they opt out in advance under Bilt’s rules. Verify your account timing before comparing the recurring credits or counting Flexible Bilt Cash.

## Renters and homeowners should run one more comparison

For an eligible $3,000 rent or mortgage payment with $1,500 of qualifying everyday spend in the same billing cycle, the housing-only option earns 2,250 points at 0.75x. At 1¢ per point that is $22.50 for that cycle. Those points can also be earned with Blue or Obsidian if the same conditions are met. Compare the rewards on the $1,500 of purchases against what your current card already earns. If that card earns 3% in the relevant categories, routing the full $1,500 to Palladium at 2x costs $15 in purchase rewards under the same valuation.

## Add Palladium or replace Venture X?

[Capital One Venture X](/cards/capital-one-venture-x) charges $395 and earns 2 miles per dollar on ordinary purchases. At an illustrative 1¢ per Bilt Point and 1¢ per Capital One mile, the ordinary purchase rewards tie. Ignore welcome offers and portal multipliers, and assume your current housing payment earns zero rewards. If the $3,000 housing and $1,500 qualifying spend example repeats for 12 billing cycles, Palladium’s housing points are worth $270 for the year.

| Decision | How much usable Palladium benefit would cover the gap? |
| --- | --- |
| Keep Venture X and add Palladium | $225. Palladium adds a $495 fee and $270 in housing rewards. Count only new benefits, since you keep Venture X either way. |
| Replace Venture X at renewal | $30 if you would give up $200 of usable Venture X benefits. Palladium adds $100 in annual fees and $270 in housing rewards, while replacing the $200 in benefits. |

The $200 is an example of the value the household actually gets from Venture X each year. Include lounge visits or other perks only to the extent the decision changes their value. If you already earn housing rewards, use the difference in housing value. If Blue or Obsidian could earn the same housing points for less, compare that option too.

Palladium is a poor fit if you mainly want a higher housing multiplier, already cover your lounge visits, cannot replace existing spending with eligible credits, or already earn better purchase rewards than 2x at your redemption value.

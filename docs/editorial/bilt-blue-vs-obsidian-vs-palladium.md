# Bilt Blue vs. Obsidian vs. Palladium

**Editorial draft · Terms checked November 21, 2026**

Bilt Blue is the lowest-cost way to try the housing rewards program. Obsidian can make sense if one of its two 3x categories captures a large share of your card spend. Palladium needs substantial spending outside Obsidian’s bonus categories, useful premium benefits, or both. All three use the same housing tiers under the housing-only option, so a higher annual fee alone buys no higher housing multiplier.

## Compare the three Bilt cards

| Card | Annual fee | Standard earn rates on eligible purchases | Annual benefits before the 2027 refresh | Annual benefits after the refresh |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| [Bilt Blue](/cards/bilt-blue) | $0 | 1x | No recurring credit counted here | No recurring credit counted here |
| [Bilt Obsidian](/cards/bilt-obsidian) | $95 | 3x on dining or groceries by choice, 2x on travel, 1x elsewhere. Grocery bonus capped at $25,000 a year. | Up to $100 in Bilt Travel hotel credits in two $50 periods | $100 in Bilt Cash |
| [Bilt Palladium](/cards/bilt-palladium) | $495 | 2x | Up to $400 in Bilt Travel hotel credits in two $200 periods, plus $200 in Bilt Cash | $600 in Bilt Cash, with eligible hotel redemptions of up to $200 twice a year within that balance |

Palladium also includes Priority Pass. Value it based on visits you expect to use that your existing cards do not already cover. Bilt Cash is restricted to eligible redemptions. The refreshed Palladium hotel allowance comes out of its Bilt Cash balance. It adds no second credit.

## How to compare housing points with your current card

Eligible rent and mortgage payments through Bilt draw from a linked bank account. They carry no transaction fee with the card. Under the housing-only option, the same billing cycle’s qualifying everyday spend sets the rate on the eligible housing payment.

| Qualifying spend as a share of housing payment | Housing points |
| --- | ---: |
| Below 25% | 250 points |
| 25% to under 50% | 0.5x |
| 50% to under 75% | 0.75x |
| 75% to under 100% | 1x |
| 100% or more | 1.25x |

For example, $1,000 of qualifying spending and a $2,000 eligible rent payment produce 1,500 housing points in the housing-only option. At an illustrative 1¢ per point, that is $15. Moving the $1,000 from a 2% card to Blue at 1x costs $10 in purchase rewards. The incremental gain is $5 for that cycle versus the 2% card and a housing payment earning zero. A household that already earns housing points should subtract those as well.

The same math applies to an eligible mortgage. A larger mortgage increases the spending needed to reach a given tier. For a $4,000 mortgage, keeping the same $1,000 in qualifying card spend reaches 0.5x and earns 2,000 housing points. Moving another $1,000 to reach 0.75x raises housing rewards by 1,000 points. At 1¢ per point, the extra $10 just offsets the additional $10 lost by switching from 2% cash back to Blue.

## When Obsidian or Palladium earns its fee

These examples value points at 1¢ and exclude housing, credits, lounges, welcome offers, and partner multipliers. Each assumes the same spending goes on the card being compared.

| Annual spending | Blue after fee | Obsidian after fee | Palladium after fee |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $12,000 eligible dining plus $24,000 other eligible spend | $360 | $505 | $225 |
| $6,000 eligible dining plus $54,000 other eligible spend | $600 | $625 | $705 |

Against Blue, Obsidian earns 2 additional points per dollar in its chosen 3x category. At 1¢ per point and with no usable credit or other bonus spending, $4,750 of eligible spending covers the $95 fee. This changes if your current card already earns 3% on dining or if you redeem Bilt Points below 1¢.

Palladium’s 2x on ordinary purchases adds 1 point per dollar over Blue. At 1¢ per point it takes $49,500 in eligible purchases to cover the $495 fee on that difference alone. A no-fee 2% card remains a separate comparison. It matches Palladium’s 2x everyday rate at this valuation before allowing for the fee, housing points, or usable benefits.

## Check which terms apply to you

Accounts opened on or after January 1, 2027 enter refreshed housing-only rewards. Earlier accounts generally move at their first anniversary in 2027. If Flexible Bilt Cash is your active option, you can elect to keep it and your existing credits in the Bilt app before that anniversary. After making that election, you can move to the refreshed terms later, effective the next billing cycle. That move is permanent. Compare each version’s annual credits using purchases you already plan to make.

### Compare the two options before your anniversary

Take the same $2,000 eligible rent payment and $1,000 in qualifying purchases. Hold the card and purchases constant, so the usual purchase points and annual fee cancel out. Assume no starting Bilt Cash balance and set aside the different annual credits above.

| Option | Rewards from the example | Estimated value at 1¢ per point |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Housing-only | 1,500 housing points at the 50% spending threshold | $15 |
| Flexible Bilt Cash, redeemed for housing points | $40 Bilt Cash earned at 4%. At 3¢ Bilt Cash per point, redeem $39.99 for 1,333 housing points. | $13.33, with 1¢ Bilt Cash left |

If you use the $40 for other eligible redemptions, those redemptions need to replace at least $15 of planned spending to match the housing-only example. That is 37.5¢ of real value per dollar of Bilt Cash. Count the balance once. Any Bilt Cash spent to unlock housing points is no longer available for other redemptions.
